# visual_style_rules

## Purpose
This file defines the working visual style rules for JVL ECHO.
Use it for ad creative, landing pages, Amazon assets, product-page imagery, motion assets, and creative briefs.

## Core visual principle
ECHO should look like a real adult-home leisure product that is emotionally inviting, clearly understood, quietly premium, and easy to trust.

The visual system should:
- open with nostalgia or togetherness
- clarify the category quickly
- show function and ownership proof early
- make the product feel believable in a real adult home
- avoid both gamer-clutter energy and flashy luxury energy

## Why this matters
The current product image is often read too generically - as a gaming device, monitor, screen, or family-play object rather than clearly as a premium home arcade.
Because of that, visuals must actively improve category clarity, proof, and adult-home meaning.

## Visual priority stack
Use this order for creative decisions whenever possible:

1. Demo-style function proof
- Show the machine working
- Show taps, gameplay motion, and immediate understandability

2. Detail close-ups
- Controls
- Screen clarity
- Materials
- Finish
- Build quality

3. Size / scale proof
- Show how it fits on real furniture and in real rooms
- Make proportions obvious

4. Real-customer or believable in-use proof
- Credible ownership realism
- Natural use, not overacted performance

5. Adult-home context
- Quiet, warm, mature environments
- Product belongs in the room, not just in a showroom

## First visual rule
If the asset is static, the visual or headline must make the category obvious.
Do not rely on silhouette alone.
When needed, pair visuals with clear category labels such as:
- home arcade
- bartop arcade
- touchscreen arcade for home
- arcade machine for home
- all-in-one arcade

## Environment rules
Preferred settings:
- den
- basement lounge
- home bar
- game room
- cabin
- lake-house style leisure space

The room should feel:
- adult
- lived-in
- tasteful
- warm
- believable
- comfortable

## Lighting and mood
Use:
- warm ambient light
- believable home lighting
- moderate contrast
- premium restraint

Avoid:
- harsh showroom lighting
- over-dramatic theatrical lighting
- childish arcade-neon overload
- over-stylized glow that makes the product feel gimmicky

## What to show
### Always helpful
- the machine clearly visible
- readable product shape
- real scale in the room
- believable use posture
- close-ups of screen, controls, finish, and materials
- function cues that explain how it works

### Strong supporting visuals
- believable family / friends / guests scenes
- calm solo-use moments
- side-by-side play if relevant
- room-fit context
- real-world surfaces and furniture

## What not to show
- noisy gamer-bedroom aesthetic
- childish playroom energy
- generic tech-clutter framing
- unrealistic luxury mansion cues
- mood-only lifestyle scenes with weak product clarity
- over-polished fake reflections
- over-cropped product shots that make the object ambiguous
- gaming-neon clichés unless intentionally used in a narrow concept

## Styling rules
The product should feel:
- quietly premium
- adult-home friendly
- solid
- durable
- warm
- restrained

The product should not feel:
- flashy
- status-hungry
- toy-like
- gimmicky
- cheap
- disposable

## People in visuals
If people are shown:
- they should feel believable for the target audience
- use should feel casual and natural
- shared scenes should feel like real home interaction, not staged excitement
- expressions and body language should feel relaxed, warm, and grounded

Preferred people scenarios:
- couple at home
- family shared play
- friends / guests in a game-room or home-bar setting
- calm solo decompression scene

## Branding in generated images — hard rules

Generated images are not product photography. Three things go wrong without a
rule, and all three have happened:

**No third-party brands, logos, or intellectual property. Ever.**
A generated bar scene came back with a cabinet marked PANDORA'S BOX — a real
competitor — running a game list of licensed SNK titles. Another put Marvel and
film posters on the wall. `claims_constraints.md` forbids trademarked game
titles in text; the same rule holds for anything visible in an image, including
screens, cabinet art, posters, packaging and signage. If a machine appears, its
cabinet and screen carry no readable brand, no recognisable character, and no
identifiable game.

**Editorial articles show unbranded machines above the product section.**
An article analysing whether an arcade machine pays for itself must not be
illustrated with our own machine at the top. That makes the product look like
the subject of the analysis rather than one option in it, and a reader who
notices stops trusting the numbers. Generic, unbranded venue and cabinet
imagery everywhere except the closing product section, which may show the real
product properly.

**Never generate an image of a table, a chart, or anything made of numbers.**
An image model draws something that looks like a table; it does not reproduce
the figures. A generated "comparison of three payback scenarios" came back
showing $1,000 and $1,500 a week for an article whose scenarios were $30, $75
and $160 — wrong by a factor of thirteen, sitting beside the real markdown table
it duplicated. Numbers inside an image are also unreadable to screen readers,
uncopyable, and invisible to search. Tables belong in markdown. If the shape of
a relationship genuinely needs showing, that is a chart built from the data, not
a picture of one — propose it and leave it to be produced properly.

**A caption may not name the product outside the closing product section.**
An editorial article mentions the machine once, in its own section at the end.
A caption is part of the article, and putting "ECHO HD3 Commercial Edition"
under a picture halfway up puts the product back where the rules just removed
it — twice now, in consecutive runs. Above that closing section a caption
describes what is in the frame in generic terms and names nothing.

**Captions describe, they do not sell.**
A caption states what is in the frame. It does not assert specifications, name
editions, or make performance claims — the image cannot evidence any of that,
and captions are read as fact rather than as marketing. "A touchscreen arcade
cabinet on a bar counter" is a caption. "ECHO Commercial is built for bars and
event venues" is an advertisement in a place the reader does not expect one.

## Realism rules
- Preserve realistic proportions
- Preserve realistic materials
- Preserve realistic room scale
- Preserve realistic reflections and textures
- Do not over-retouch into fake perfection

## Asset recommendations by use case
### Discovery / top of funnel
Prioritize:
- nostalgia-led hero
- togetherness-led hero
- clear category read
- fast emotional hook

### Evaluation / mid funnel
Prioritize:
- demo video
- room scale
- close-up quality proof
- in-use realism

### Conversion / bottom funnel
Prioritize:
- detail close-ups
- size proof
- what's-in-the-box support visuals
- quality and finish proof
- support / warranty reassurance layers where relevant

## Final shorthand
The visual system should make ECHO feel like nostalgia made real for an adult home - clear, credible, warm, quietly premium, and easy to imagine owning for years.
